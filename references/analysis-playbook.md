# Analysis playbook

## 1. Emergent taxonomy

Read representative records, propose candidate categories from repeated meanings, define boundaries, classify the corpus, review ambiguous items, then merge categories that are too small or indistinguishable. Preserve the category definition and representative row IDs.

## 2. Coverage–performance gap

For each content category, calculate publication count/share, mean reach, median reach, engagement rate when denominators are comparable, and top-decile share. Mean alone is vulnerable to viral outliers; always compare it with median and sample size.

Flag categories with high publishing share but below-baseline performance, and categories with low supply but above-baseline performance. Treat these as hypotheses, not causal proof.

## 3. Voice of customer

Exclude official replies from customer-demand counts. Count specific asks such as price, sizing, compatibility, installation, delivery, warranty, error codes, and expansion only after they emerge in the corpus. Report `n/N`, representative paraphrases, and identity confidence.

## 4. Response behavior

Measure the share of questions receiving useful answers, generic templates, channel redirection, or no visible reply. Distinguish “call/DM us” from an answer that resolves the question publicly.

## 5. Opportunity map

Cross customer demand with competitor supply and response quality:

- high demand + low content supply → content opportunity;
- high demand + weak public answers → service/technical-authority opportunity;
- high supply + weak performance → reduce or redesign;
- high performance + low supply → test more frequently.

## Claim format

Write each important point as:

1. observation with count and denominator;
2. cautious interpretation;
3. recommended experiment;
4. row IDs or source URLs that support it.

Never infer market share, sales, or causality from social engagement alone.
