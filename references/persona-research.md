# Evidence-grounded market personas

Use this playbook to turn a declared public corpus into decision-support roles for installers, EPCs, resellers, or end customers. It does not create a statistically representative population model or a fictional “average buyer.”

## Research contract

Declare the market, product boundary, source accounts/forums, date range, language, relevance rules, role questions, and intended decision. For mixed content, state separate strata such as recent twelve-month content and historical high-reach content; do not silently mix them.

## Build the evidence set

Preserve content and conversations with stable IDs, source links, dates, visible metrics, translations, and identity confidence. Exclude unrelated product categories before role analysis. Keep creator/installer statements, end-customer questions, and official replies separate.

## Derive roles from repeated trade-offs

Read the complete declared corpus, then group repeated combinations of:

- decision criteria, requested proof, and technical vocabulary;
- product, installation, support, price, delivery, warranty, or training concerns;
- preferred customer-facing explanations and content formats;
- observed negative signals, their severity, recurrence, recency, and source confidence.

Create two or three distinct roles only when the evidence supports distinct trade-offs. Name roles after their observable orientation, not a real person or company. Each role requires supporting IDs, counterexamples, sample size, and a confidence label.

## Required persona card

```text
Role orientation: [evidence-grounded label]
Population and scope: [sources, dates, n]
Decision job: [what this role is trying to decide]
Priorities and proof required: [with evidence IDs]
Language used with customers: [paraphrased phrases and IDs]
Negative signals: [severity, recurrence, evidence IDs]
What content/product claim this role would challenge: [testable review rule]
Counterevidence and limitation: [what does not fit or remains uncertain]
```

## Use the personas safely

Use the cards to review a PPT, Flyer, post, support article, or feature proposition. A useful output is a list of evidence-backed objections, missing proof, and testable wording changes. Do not present the personas as customer research, market share evidence, or a substitute for interviews and surveys.
